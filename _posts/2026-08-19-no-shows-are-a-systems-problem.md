A no-show is worse than an inquiry you never got. You paid to get the lead, you answered it, you held the slot, you turned down other work for it, and then you got nothing.

Most owners treat this as a character flaw in customers. It usually isn't. People book things three weeks out, on a phone, between other tasks, and then life happens to them. The businesses with low no-show rates aren't serving better people. They built a system.

## The reminder evidence is unusually solid

Appointment reminders are one of the few operational tactics with proper randomized-trial evidence behind them, because healthcare has been studying the problem for decades.

A **2013 Cochrane systematic review** of mobile phone messaging reminders for healthcare appointments found attendance improved from roughly **67.8% with no reminder to about 78.6% with a text reminder.** That is an enormous effect for something that costs a fraction of a cent to send.

A separate 2013 randomized trial published in *BMC Health Services Research* compared text reminders against telephone reminders in a primary care clinic and found text messaging performed comparably to phone calls, at substantially lower cost.

Two honest caveats. This is healthcare, where the appointment is usually months out and often unpleasant; your trade appointment may not behave identically. And a reminder can't fix a booking someone regretted the moment they made it. But the direction is well-established and the mechanism is obvious: most no-shows are forgetting, not deciding.

## What a reminder sequence should actually look like

Not "more reminders." A specific, small number, each doing a different job.

**Immediately on booking, the confirmation.** This is the most-read message you will ever send, and most businesses waste it. It should contain: date and time in plain words ("Thursday, October 2, at 10:00am"), where, who is coming, roughly how long, what they need to have ready, what it costs or what the deposit covers, and how to reschedule in one tap.

**24 hours before, the reminder.** Short. Time, place, and a reschedule link. That's it.

**A couple of hours before, for a visit-based service, the "on my way" window.** "Marcus will be there between 10 and 11." This one kills the anxious cancellation, where somebody bails because they don't know if you're actually coming.

Three messages, each with a job. A fourth is nagging, and nagging trains people to ignore you.

## The reschedule link is the whole trick

Here is the shift that changes the number: **make rescheduling easier than not showing up.**

Right now, for most businesses, a customer who realizes at 8am that they can't make 10am has two options: call and have an awkward conversation with a person, or just not be there. The second option costs them nothing socially. So they take it, and you learn about it by standing in a driveway.

Put a one-tap reschedule link in every message. You are not "making it easy to cancel." You are converting a silent no-show into a rebooked job and an open slot you can still fill.

## The other three levers

**Shorten the gap.** No-show risk grows with lead time. If you're booking three weeks out, that's a capacity problem wearing a no-show costume, and a waitlist that can fill a hole on two hours' notice is worth more than a fourth reminder.

**Take something.** A deposit, a card on file, or even a required confirmation tap changes behavior, because it converts a vague intention into a commitment. Match the size to the job: a $250 deposit on a $9,000 install is normal; a deposit on a $60 consult reads as distrust and costs you bookings. Pick one, say it clearly at booking time, and never surprise anyone with it.

**Reply to the reply.** If a reminder sends from a number nobody monitors, people who *try* to reschedule get nothing back and become no-shows anyway. A reminder system that can't receive a message is half a system.

## Measure it, once a month

Two numbers:

1. **No-show rate**. Booked appointments where nobody was there, as a share of all booked appointments.
2. **Reschedule rate**. Appointments moved before the start time.

You want the first low and the second *healthy*. A business with a 0% reschedule rate and a 14% no-show rate has made rescheduling too hard. Moving those customers from column one to column two is the cheapest revenue available to you this month.
